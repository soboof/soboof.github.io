01 · Origin

From Tehran
to triangles.

A path that moved through civil engineering, graphic design, and eventually into three-dimensional form — driven by one question: what can geometry hold?
CH · I · TEHRAN
CH · I · 1998 → 2018

Civil roots, visual pull.

Architecture teaches you that structure and beauty are the same thing.

Growing up in Tehran, Soby initially pursued Civil Engineering — studying structure, load, and the logic of built space. But what held her attention was never the numbers: it was the way a form catches light. The decision to redirect toward visual design was not a detour — it was a sharper aim at the same target.

CH · II · NETHERLANDS
CH · II · 2019 → NOW

Leeuwarden: studio opens.

A new country. The same obsession with form.

Moving to the Netherlands to study Creative Business opened both a professional framework and a physical studio space. Graphic design expanded into 3D design, then into animation, then into something that demanded to be held: hand-crafted sculptural objects where digital precision meets ancient technique.

02 · Practice

The Mirbreak
method.

Modular 3D-printed geometric bodies. Hand-cut mirror tiles. A centuries-old Persian technique applied to contemporary sculptural form.
AYENEH · KARI
TECHNIQUE · PERSIAN CRAFT

Mirrors on geometry.

Each triangular face captures a different fragment of the world.

Ayeneh-Kari — the ancient Persian art of mirror-mosaic — is applied here not to mosque ceilings but to 3D-printed modular bodies. Each tile is hand-cut, scored, and mounted so that every face of the geometric form becomes a tiny mirror: reflecting the room, the light, the viewer. The result is a figure that is never the same twice.

MODULAR · SYSTEM
SYSTEM · DIGITAL + HAND

Modular logic,
organic result.

Order is the starting point, not the destination.

Each sculpture begins with a fixed geometric primitive — icosahedron, tetrahedron, pyramid — 3D-printed from a modular system. From there, the hand takes over: the mirror tiling breaks the rigid module into something alive. Fragile yet precise. Abstract shapes that feel both ancient and contemporary. Structures that reflect their surroundings.

04 · Philosophy

What the work
holds.

The values embedded in every decision — material, form, system, and finish.
"

These figures are reflections of ourselves and our surroundings — fragile yet precise geometric structures that hold a triangular fragment of reality on each face, shifting with every point of view.

— Soby Farahat · Soboof Studio · Leeuwarden
Value · 01
Sustainability
Objects made to last. Materials chosen for longevity, not convenience. A Mirbreak sculpture is designed to be inherited.
Value · 02
Individuality
No two pieces are identical. The same modular logic produces a different result every time — order enabling diversity, not erasing it.
Value · 03
Participation
The workshop model is not secondary — it is part of the philosophy. Art that can be passed on, taught, and made collectively.
Value · 04
Continuity
Ancient technique, contemporary form. The Ayeneh-Kari tradition does not belong in a museum — it belongs in a living studio practice.
