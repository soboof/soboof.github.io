01 · Origin

From load paths
to triangles.

A path that ran through civil engineering and graphic design before it arrived at three-dimensional form, driven by one question: what can geometry be made to hold?
CH · I · ENGINEERING
CH · I · ENGINEERING

Civil roots, visual pull.

Structure first, then the question of what structure is for.

Soby started in Civil Engineering in Tehran: load paths, tolerances, the logic of built space. The training stuck; the interest moved. What held her attention was never the calculation, it was the way a form takes light and how much a single repeated unit can be made to do. She redirected into visual design and kept the engineering habits: measure first, then build.

CH · II · NETHERLANDS
CH · II · LEEUWARDEN

A studio in the north.

A new country, a workbench, and a printer running overnight.

Moving to the Netherlands for Creative Business gave the work a commercial frame and, more to the point, a room to make things in. Graphic design widened into 3D design and motion, then into objects that have to be held to be understood. Soboof works out of Leeuwarden today, taking commissions and releasing its own tools.

02 · The studio

One studio,
four outputs.

Soboof is not a single product, and the four projects do not share a technique. What they share is who they are aimed at: the person holding the piece, printing it, or building with it.
AYENEH · KARI
01 · MIRBREAK · SCULPTURE

Mirrored sculpture.

Eleven pieces so far, and each one reflects a different room.

Hand-cut mirror tiles laid over modular 3D-printed bodies in the Ayeneh-Kari technique, a Persian craft used for centuries on palace and shrine ceilings. Here it goes onto printed geometry instead. Every face holds a triangular fragment of the room, so the piece changes as the viewer moves around it. Work is one-of-a-kind or small edition, made by hand in Leeuwarden.

HAND PRESS · LAB
02 · HAND PRESS LAB · PRINT

Death Culture.

A printing kiosk where the visitor lays the body bags on the sheet.

An ongoing hand-printing project with Ju Bergman: linocut stamps of cypress, dogs, birds of prey and mountains, printed on site rather than sold finished. Sixty-eight sheets exist so far, across four sets. The subject is how intolerance ends in a culture of death, and the kiosk is built so that the conversation, not the print, is what people leave with.

MODULAR · SYSTEM
03 · PYTHAGORAS ENGINE · SOFTWARE

The geometry, as software.

Free in the browser. The bodies behind Mirbreak are grown in it.

The Pythagoras Engine is a procedural polyhedron builder: snap tetrahedra, Johnson solids, prisms and cupolas face to face, set height and rotation, then export the form you grew. It began as the studio's own modelling step and was released as a free web tool, on the view that the rule is worth more shared than any single object it produces.

SUPERADOBE · DOMES
04 · SUPERADOBE · PROCEDURAL DESIGN

Shelter, costed.

Earthbag domes, down to the running metre of sack.

The SuperAdobe Generator is a separate strand of the studio, and a free and open-source one: a designer for earthbag construction. Lay domes, vaults and cylinders over a scan of your own land, watch the structure rise course by course, and get back the sack, wire, fill and plaster it will take. Procedural design pointed at shelter people can actually build.

04 · Philosophy

What the work
holds.

The values sitting behind every decision. Each one answers the same question: who is this for, and what does it ask of them?
"

These figures are reflections of ourselves and our surroundings: fragile yet precise geometric structures that hold a triangular fragment of reality on each face, shifting with every point of view.

Soby Farahat · Soboof Studio · Leeuwarden
Value · 01
Sustainability
Objects made to last. Materials chosen for longevity rather than convenience. A Mirbreak sculpture is meant to be inherited.
Value · 02
Embodied social engagement
The workshop and the printing kiosk are not side projects, they are the argument. People are handed the tools and put to work, so the piece is made with them rather than sold to them.
Value · 03
Individuality
No two pieces are identical. One modular logic gives a different result every time: order that enables difference instead of erasing it.
Value · 04
Access
Two of the four projects are given away free, and the workshop hands over the tools rather than the object. What the studio can make alone will always reach fewer people than what others can make with it.
