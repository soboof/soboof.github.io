02 · Working method

Three chapters

Every Mirbreak sculpture moves through the same three stages: drawn as geometry, printed as a body, then tiled by hand until it reflects.
CH · I · 3D DESIGN
CHAPTER · 01

Drawn in the Factory, grown in the Builder.

Tetrahedrons, dodecahedrons, Johnson solids: given joints, then told to multiply.

Every piece starts in the studio's own software, the Pythagoras Engine. In the Factory a single module is drawn: a base polyhedron (triangular, pentagonal, hexagonal and up) whose every face is assigned a role, left open or closed or cut as a pin, tunnel, bolt or plug. That is the joint the next module will land on.

The Builder then grows the sculpture from those parts, one connection face, height and rotation at a time. A fixed structural logic branches into roots, mycelial networks, crystalline growth; rigid repetition becoming dynamic multiplicity, order as the seed of individuality.

CH · II · PRINTING
CHAPTER · 02

Printed in recycled plastic.

The geometry leaves the screen and becomes a body you can hold.

The assembled geometry is exported and printed module by module in recycled 3D-printed plastic, then joined along the same faces the Factory prepared for it. Nothing is carved down to shape; the print is the structure.

What comes off the bed is the raw body of the sculpture: matte, faceted, and still blind. Where a piece calls for it, black marble or metal enters here too.

CH · III · MIRROR
CHAPTER · 03

Tiled by hand in cut mirror.

Ayeneh-Kari: a centuries-old craft, laid over a printed geometry.

Each face is then tiled with hand-cut mirrored glass using the centuries-old Persian Ayeneh-Kari technique. It is the slowest chapter and the only one done entirely by hand.

Once tiled, the object stops being a surface and starts being a window: every facet catches a triangular fragment of the room and shifts with the viewer's point of view. The sculpture is finished when it reflects: a living system shaped by light, space, and perception.
